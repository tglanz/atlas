# Practitioner

## Browse Movements

Find Movements in the Movement Index by name, with suggestions while typing, and by toggling tags.

## View a Movement

View its description, items grouped by type (such as cues and comments), illustrations, tags, and the Movements related to it. Each relationship type appears in its own subsection, such as Progressions, with previews of candidate Movements.

## Add a Movement to a Routine

From a Movement, choose a Routine and one of its Sections, or create a Routine with the Movement already selected. If the Routine has no Sections, adding its first Movement creates a Practice section. Authentication continues to the same choice after sign-in or registration.

## Create a Routine

Create a Routine with a name and description, organized into ordered Sections containing Movements.

## Edit a Routine

Update the Routine details, manage its Sections, and reorder or replace Movements.

## View a Routine

Follow the Routine through its ordered Sections and Movements, with each Movement's name, Illustrations and Cues visible inline.

## Share a Routine

The owner creates a public link from the Routine view and copies it for others. Anyone with the link can read the current Routine without signing in. The owner can disable the link at any time; a later share creates a different link. Sharing does not enable editing or discoverability in the public Movement catalog.

# Maintainer

## Create a Movement

Create a Movement with its name, description, items, and tags. Relationships and Illustrations can be added at the same time or afterwards.

## Edit a Movement

Change a Movement's details, relationships, or Illustrations. The change is visible immediately.

## Relate Movements

In the Movement editor, define the Movement's relationships to other existing Movements, each with a type chosen from the offered types, such as progression or variant. Adding a relationship also adds its inverse on the other Movement, for example a regression for a progression. A Movement shows the Movements it is related to in one section per type, such as Progressions.

## Set Movement Illustrations

One Illustration is produced for each stage of the Movement. The stages are the Movement's own and are edited with it.

1. In the Movement editor, choose an Avatar and a Viewpoint (the default Viewpoint is preselected) and press Generate. The Movement must be saved first, and only one generation can run for it at a time.
2. Each stage shows its progress, and its image as soon as it is ready.
3. When every image is ready, they are shown for review. Approving replaces the Movement's Illustrations; discarding leaves them unchanged.
4. If a stage fails, the generation stops and shows why. The maintainer can start a new one.

Saving a Movement never changes its Illustrations.
